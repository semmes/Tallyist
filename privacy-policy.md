# Tallyist Privacy Policy

**Last updated: September 24, 2026**

Tallyist does not collect your data. Nothing you log leaves your control.

This page is the authoritative version of the policy for the Tallyist app on
iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. The same policy ships inside the iPhone and iPad
app, under Settings → About → Privacy Policy. It stays in English even where
the app's interface is translated, so that what it claims can be checked
against the app's privacy manifests and entitlements.

## What Tallyist stores

- The drinks you log: type, size, strength, when, and the region you logged
  each one under.
- Days recorded as alcohol-free — by you, or by another app that recorded zero
  drinks in Apple Health.
- Your settings, such as which region's standard-drink definition your totals use.
- A short diagnostic record, such as when your log last synced, when a widget
  last logged a drink, and when the app last read from Apple Health. It holds
  none of the figures read for Apple Health on Trends, and it is never sent
  anywhere.

All of it is stored on your device, in the app's own storage. If you use
Tallyist on Apple Watch, the watch keeps its own copy of your log in the same
way. There are no accounts and no sign-up.

## Where your data can go — always under your control

- **iCloud.** If your device is signed into iCloud, your log syncs through your
  own *private* iCloud database so it follows you across your devices. That
  database belongs to your Apple Account; the developer cannot read it, and no
  server other than Apple's is involved. Sync stops if you sign out, and the
  app keeps working locally. Your settings are not synced through iCloud.
- **Apple Health.** With your permission, drinks you log are saved to Health as
  "alcoholic beverages", and Tallyist reads that same category back — including
  drinks that other apps recorded there, which then appear in your Tallyist log,
  clearly labeled, and days they recorded as zero drinks, which appear as
  no-alcohol days. You can grant, refuse, or revoke this at any time in the
  Health app, or in the Settings app under Privacy & Security.
- **Apple Health on Trends.** Only if you turn it on, Tallyist also reads four
  other kinds of Health data: resting heart rate, sleep, heart rate
  variability, and sleeping wrist temperature, along with the unit your Health
  app shows temperature in. Each has its own switch in Settings, under Apple
  Health on Trends, and all four are off until you turn them on there or from
  a card on Trends that asks once. The data is read on your device when Trends
  shows it, used only to show your own averages on nights you logged drinks
  and nights you recorded as no alcohol, and then discarded. None of it is
  saved in the app, synced to iCloud, written back to Health, or sent
  anywhere. You can turn a switch off, or revoke access in Health, at any
  time. Tallyist reads no other Health data.
- **Apple Watch.** If you use Tallyist on Apple Watch, your iPhone sends two
  settings straight to your paired watch: your region and what the plus button
  logs, with the time it sent them. Nothing else about you travels that way,
  and no drink ever does; your log moves between your devices only through
  iCloud, as above. The watch app reads and writes no Health data. Drinks you
  log on the watch are saved to Health by Tallyist on your iPhone or iPad, if
  you allow it there, the next time you open it after they have synced.
- **Widgets and complications.** The home-screen widget shares the app's
  storage on the same iPhone or iPad, and the watch complications share the watch app's
  storage on your watch. Nothing about that sharing leaves the device.
- **Export and sharing.** Settings → Export log turns your whole record into a
  CSV file, and the calendar can render a month or a year as an image. Both are
  created on your device, only when you ask, and go only where you send them
  through the system share sheet. The app keeps no copy, adds no identifier, and
  does not record whether or where you shared anything.

## What Tallyist does not do

- No analytics, and no crash-reporting SDKs.
- No advertising, and no tracking of any kind.
- No third-party services. The app contains no networking code of its own; the
  only network traffic related to Tallyist is Apple's iCloud sync described
  above, the two settings your iPhone sends to your own watch, and, if you
  open the tip jar or leave a tip, Apple's own App Store product and purchase
  processing.
- No selling, sharing, or transfer of your data to anyone, because the
  developer never has it in the first place.

In App Store terms: **Data Not Collected**.

## Tips

Tallyist has an optional tip jar (Settings → Buy me a drink). Payments are
processed entirely by Apple through your App Store account, exactly like any
App Store purchase: Tallyist never sees your payment details, and Apple tells
the app only that a purchase completed. Tips unlock nothing, and nothing about
tipping — or not — appears in or affects your drink log. Recurring tips can be
cancelled any time in your App Store subscription settings, and the app offers
a local reminder a week before each renewal so you can cancel before being
charged.

## Deleting your data

- Any entry you logged can be deleted in the app, individually. A drink or a
  no-alcohol day that another app recorded in Apple Health is deleted in that
  app or in the Health app, and Tallyist follows.
- Deleting the app removes everything it stored on that device. On Apple
  Watch, deleting the watch app removes the watch's copy.
- iCloud copies can be removed in the iOS Settings app under your Apple
  Account → iCloud → Manage Account Storage.
- Data saved to Health is yours in Health: delete it there under Browse →
  Other Data → Alcohol Consumption. Nothing Tallyist reads for Apple Health on
  Trends is ever stored, so there is nothing of it to delete.

## Changes to this policy

This policy is kept under version control; every change to it, and its
date, is recorded. If a future version of the app ever
collects data — for example, if a server component is added — this policy and
the App Store privacy labels will change *before* that version ships.
